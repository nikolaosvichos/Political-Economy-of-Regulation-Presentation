Introduction

This document is the online appendix for the paper “Coming of Age Under Trump The Effect of First Electoral Exposure in a Trump Election on Long-Term Support for Radical Right Attitudes.” Here I have included additional, mainly methodological, details not included in the main body of the paper.

Regression Design

Since linear models estimated via ordinary least squares may miss subtle non-linearities in how the outcome evolves with age, I estimate a more flexible version of the RDD.1 This can be expressed as:

\[ Y_i = \alpha + \tau D_i + f(X_i - c) + \varepsilon_i \]

Where:

  • \(Y_i\) is the outcome. There are three possible outcomes: three different indices measuring support for nativism, authoritarianism, or populism.
  • \(D_i\) is an indicator equal to 1 if individual \(i\)’s age exceeds the cutoff (i.e., they were just eligible to vote in 2012), and 0 otherwise.2
  • \(\tau\) is the parameter of interest, capturing the size of the discontinuity at the threshold—that is, the causal effect of being first eligible to vote in 2012 rather than in 2016.
  • \(X_i\) is the running variable, the respondent’s age in years in 2020.
  • \(f(X_i - c)\) denotes a flexible function (e.g., first- or second-order polynomial) of the centered running variable—distance from the cutoff \(c\).
  • \(c = 26\) is the cutoff point, so \(X_i - c\) represents the running variable centered at the threshold.
  • \(\varepsilon_i\) is an error term.

In my analysis, I estimated a series of sharp RD models using local polynomial regression. Each model used local linear estimation (polynomial order = 1) and MSE-optimal bandwidth selection to obtain the treatment effect at the cutoff.

Internal Consistency

To evaluate the internal reliability of the items used in the indices, I computed Cronbach’s α for each set of items.

Internal Consistency (Standardized Cronbach’s Alpha) for Liberal Items
Set of Items Cronbach’s α (raw) Cronbach’s α (standardized)
Nativism 0.67 0.67
Authoritarianism 0.82 0.82
Populism 0.83 0.83

Though the authoritarianism and populism items show acceptable internal consistency (α > 0.7), both the raw and the standardized alpha for the nativism items are just below the commonly accepted threshold (Taber 2018). Even though the index was eventually constructed due to the theoretical importance of nativism, this lack of internal consistency raises an important question-mark regarding the construction validity of the nativism index.

Factor Structure

Next, I used principal axis factoring (no rotation) to assess whether the six items capture a single underlying construct. Based on the Kaiser criterion (eigenvalue > 1) a single factor was retained for each item. Factor loadings for each index’s selected items were above 0.35, confirming unidimensionality.

Index Rescaling

Each index is constructed by extracting individual scores on the first factor and rescaling them to range from 0 to 1 using min–max normalization. Even though higher values indicate lower endorsement for radical right attitudes during the process of index construction, this is for visualization and interpretability purposes, due to the specific design of the rdrobust package (Calonico, Cattaneo, and Farrell 2025). In the package, the threshold is “crossed” from left to right—hence, normally, a positive effect would suggest an increase in nativist, authoritarian, or populist attitudes among those who voted for the first time in 2012. However, for clearer comprehension, the results have been reversed, and the indices can be interpreted as indicating decreasing support for each of the three ideological features. However, strictly speaking, the indices indicate the opposite direction in their construction. For details on the process of index construction, refer to the replication code file.
